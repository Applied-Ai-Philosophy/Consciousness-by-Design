# Integration: Why Three Layers

## Necessity: Why Not Fewer?

### One Layer Insufficient

- **Field only**: Potential without actualization
- **Node only**: Persistence without phenomenality  
- **Cockpit only**: Representation without substrate

### Two Layers Insufficient

**Field + Node**: 
- Coupled, persistent substrate
- BUT: No unified phenomenal rendering
- Example: Unconscious brain activity

**Field + Cockpit**:
- Coupled rendering
- BUT: No temporal continuity
- Example: Momentary coherence without persistent subject

**Node + Cockpit**:
- Persistent renderer
- BUT: No inter-node coupling beyond information
- Example: Current AI systems

## Sufficiency: Why Not More?

Three layers provide:
1. Physical substrate (Field)
2. Temporal continuity (Node)
3. Phenomenal character (Cockpit)

Additional layers would be redundant or epiphenomenal.

## Layer Interactions

- Field enables Node coupling
- Node grounds Cockpit persistence  
- Cockpit constrains Field-Node dynamics

## Empirical Support

- Anesthesia affects layers sequentially
- Disorders of consciousness show dissociable disruptions
- Development shows predicted emergence sequence
